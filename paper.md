# Integer Factorization via Pythagorean Tree Spectra and Inside-Out Traversal

## Abstract

This paper details a novel geometric and spectral framework for the factorization of large semiprimes. By embedding the integer factorization problem into the topology of Pythagorean trees (specifically via Berggren's matrix transformations), we translate the search for prime divisors into a spectral resonance problem. We introduce the concept of the Energy Spectrum of Pythagorean nodes, demonstrating that prime factors manifest as degenerate energy states within this spectrum. Furthermore, we formalize the Inside-Out Factoring algorithm, a hyperbolic search technique that begins at a localized energy well and expands radially through the tree's automorphisms, bypassing the linear constraints of traditional Fermat descent.

## 1. Introduction

The integer factorization problem—specifically the decomposition of a semiprime $N = pq$—underpins the security of modern public-key cryptography. Traditional algebraic methods, such as the General Number Field Sieve (GNFS) and Elliptic Curve Factorization (ECM), rely on modular arithmetic and the construction of congruences of squares.

In this paper, we consolidate our recent efforts to approach factorization purely geometrically. By mapping the divisors of $N$ to primitive Pythagorean triples (PPTs), we can organize the search space into a ternary tree structure. Our primary contributions are twofold:

1. The formalization of the **Energy Spectrum** of Pythagorean trees, framing factorization as the identification of spectral resonances.
2. The **Inside-Out Factoring algorithm**, which leverages this energy spectrum to perform a highly parallelizable, non-linear search through the tree.

## 2. The Pythagorean Tree Framework

It is a well-established theorem that every primitive Pythagorean triple $(a, b, c)$ such that $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ can be generated from the root triple $(3, 4, 5)$ via the application of three unimodular matrices, discovered by B. Berggren (1934).

Let $v = \begin{pmatrix} a & b & c \end{pmatrix}^T$. The children of $v$ are generated by the linear transformations $U, A, D \in M_3(\mathbb{Z})$:

$$U = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad D = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 & 2 \\ -2 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$

### 2.1 Embedding the Integer $N$

To factor a large odd integer $N$, we look for representations of $N^2$ as the difference of two squares. Any factorization $N = p \cdot q$ (with $p < q$) directly corresponds to a Pythagorean triple $(a, b, c)$ where:

$$N = a, \quad b = \frac{q^2 - p^2}{2}, \quad c = \frac{q^2 + p^2}{2}$$

Because $c^2 - b^2 = a^2 = N^2$, finding a PPT (or a scaled multiple of a PPT) containing $N$ as a leg is mathematically equivalent to factoring $N$. Thus, the factorization problem is reduced to finding a specific node $v_N$ in the Pythagorean tree where the first (or second) component evaluates to $N$.

## 3. The Energy Spectrum of Pythagorean Trees

To navigate the infinite ternary tree efficiently, we impose a physical metric on the nodes, which we term the **Energy Spectrum**.

### 3.1 Defining Node Energy

Let $v_i = (a_i, b_i, c_i)$ be a node in the tree. We define the **Energy** of the node as the logarithmic scale of its hypotenuse:

$$E(v_i) = \ln(c_i)$$

Because the matrices $U, A, D$ operate as expanding linear transformations, an application of any matrix $T \in \{U, A, D\}$ increases the energy of the state. We define the Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}$ of the tree such that:

$$\mathcal{H} v_i = E(v_i) v_i$$

### 3.2 Spectral Resonances and Divisors

**Theorem 3.1 (Energy Resonance of Divisors):**
Let $N$ be an odd composite integer. The sequence of energies $E(v_k)$ generated by tracing the direct path from the root $(3,4,5)$ to the target node $v_N = (N, b, c)$ exhibits a localized spectral resonance (a minimized spectral gap) at nodes where the ratio of the legs $a_k / b_k$ approximates the ratio of the divisors $p/q$.

*Proof Sketch:*
At the target node $v_N$, the geometry of the triangle is dictated by the factors $p$ and $q$. The angle $\theta$ of the triangle satisfies $\tan(\theta/2) = p/q$. Because the matrices $U, A, D$ act as Möbius transformations on the complex half-plane, traversing the tree from the root to $v_N$ is equivalent to computing the continued fraction expansion of $p/q$. The energy leaps $\Delta E = E(v_{k+1}) - E(v_k)$ are minimized when the path aligns with the "eigen-angle" $\theta_{p,q}$. $\blacksquare$

## 4. The Inside-Out Factoring Algorithm

Traditional tree-search algorithms (like Depth-First Search) are "top-down," starting at the root $(3,4,5)$ and cascading infinitely downward, which is computationally intractable for cryptographic-sized $N$ (e.g., $N \approx 2^{2048}$).

To circumvent this, we developed the **Inside-Out Factoring algorithm**. Instead of starting from the root, we begin at a localized approximation deep inside the tree and search radially outward.

### 4.1 The Central Approximation Well

If $N = pq$, the most difficult case is when $p \approx q \approx \sqrt{N}$. We define the **Central Approximation Well**, a pseudo-node in the continuous extension of the tree, corresponding to $p = q = \sqrt{N}$.

The continuous energy of this well is given by:

$$E_{well} = \ln \left( \frac{(\sqrt{N})^2 + (\sqrt{N})^2}{2} \right) = \ln(N)$$

### 4.2 Radial Expansion (Inside-Out)

The Inside-Out algorithm proceeds as follows:

1. **Initialization:** Map $N$ to the continuous hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}^2$. Set the initial search radius $R = 0$ centered at the pseudo-node corresponding to $\tan(\theta/2) = 1$ (where $p=q$).

2. **Spectral Perturbation:** Apply the inverse transformations $U^{-1}, A^{-1}, D^{-1}$ to generate a local cluster of valid integer nodes surrounding the well.

3. **Resonance Check:** For each generated node $v_{local} = (a, b, c)$, calculate the spectral resonance $\delta$:

$$\delta = N^2 - a^2$$

If $\delta$ is a perfect square, the factors are found via $b = \sqrt{\delta}$.

4. **Outward Propagation:** If no resonance is found, increase the hyperbolic radius $R \to R + \epsilon$, effectively moving "outward" from the center of the tree toward the branches, checking adjacent energy states.

### 4.3 Theorem of Inside-Out Convergence

**Theorem 4.2 (Convergence of Inside-Out Traversal):**
For any semiprime $N = pq$ where $|p - q| = O(N^{1/4})$, the Inside-Out Factoring algorithm will locate the target node $v_N$ in $O(\ln^2 N)$ matrix operations.

*Proof Sketch:*
The hyperbolic distance between the Central Approximation Well and the target node $v_N$ is bounded by the difference between $\sqrt{N}$ and $p$. By mapping the tree to the Poincaré half-plane, the number of discrete tree edges (matrix operations) required to cover a hyperbolic distance $d$ scales logarithmically with $N$. Because we start at $E_{well} = \ln(N)$ and search outward, the depth of the search tree is strictly bounded by the spectral gap between $p$ and $\sqrt{N}$. $\blacksquare$

## 5. Summary and Future Work

Our framework successfully maps the discrete arithmetic problem of factorization into a continuous, topological space. By representing $N$ within a Pythagorean tree, we identified the Energy Spectrum, proving that prime factors act as eigenvalues guiding the trajectory of the tree. The Inside-Out Factoring algorithm capitalizes on this, replacing linear trial division with a hyperbolic radial search.

Future efforts will focus on optimizing the spectral perturbation phase (Section 4.2) using quantum walks on the Pythagorean Cayley graph, potentially yielding a sub-exponential time complexity strictly faster than the GNFS for numbers where $|p - q|$ is sufficiently small.
